A new entity, without starting over

A new LLC should not mean a new bank application from scratch. The aim is to collect your group's ownership, beneficial owners and signers once, so a new entity adds only what is new: its formation documents and EIN.

  1. Add the entityIn the web app, by a person. Never by chat or automation.
  2. The bank verifies itThe partner bank runs its business verification. Identity documents go to the bank; Coffer does not keep them.
  3. Accounts open in its nameThe new entity's accounts appear in the same view, under the same controls.

Account opening is in development and launches once partner-bank approval is in place. Timing depends on the bank's review.

Controls that hold

Most payment fraud is a controls failure: a changed bank detail, a rushed approval, one person with too much access. Coffer enforces these rules in the application and again in the database.

  • Two people on every payment, with the second behind a passkey.
  • Payments lock once confirmed. To change one, cancel and start over.
  • Every action is written to a tamper-evident audit trail.
  • Dual control on every paymentA second person, behind a passkey, in the web app only.
  • Exact-amount confirmationThe initiator confirms the amount to the cent. Unconfirmed drafts expire in minutes.
  • Immutable after confirmationAmount, parties, rail, date and memo are locked. To change one, cancel and start over.
  • Limits per person, per entityNo limits on record, no initiating. Checked at confirmation and again at approval.
  • A hard per-transfer ceilingA platform maximum, independent of bank limits. Customers can lower it, never raise it.
  • Tamper-evident audit trailAppend-only, hash-chained per customer, anchored daily outside the database.
  • No blind retriesAn ambiguous bank response stops the payment until it is resolved. Nothing is resent on a guess.
  • Automation proposes, people decideAutomation can draft a payment. Only people confirm, approve or add a payee.

A program built around your controls

Coffer is a financial technology company, not a bank. Customer funds sit in accounts at a partner bank, in each customer entity's own name. No pooled or omnibus accounts.
